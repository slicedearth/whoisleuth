# Code of Conduct

WHOISleuth welcomes contributors of all backgrounds and experience levels.
This policy applies to project issues, pull requests, discussions and other
project-managed spaces, and when representing the project publicly.

## Working together

- Be respectful, clear and constructive. Critique ideas and code, not people.
- Welcome questions and different perspectives. Explain decisions and accept
  corrections; disagreement is not misconduct.
- Respect privacy and boundaries. Use synthetic examples instead of exposing
  someone's identity, credentials or private investigation material.
- Do not harass, threaten, discriminate, make unwanted sexual advances, publish
  private information without consent, or retaliate against someone for reporting
  a concern.

## Reporting a concern

Use the [contact page](https://www.whoisleuth.com/contact), choose **Privacy
request**, and use **Community conduct** as the subject. After verification,
review and send the prepared email from your own email client; the page does
not submit a report itself. Include the relevant project links, what happened
and any immediate safety concern. Share only the information needed to assess
the report. Do not open a public issue containing a conduct complaint or
someone else's private information.

Reports are handled privately, with information shared only as needed to
assess and address the concern or meet legal obligations. Confidentiality
cannot be guaranteed. Software vulnerabilities use the separate
[security reporting process](SECURITY.md).

## Responding fairly

Maintainers assess the circumstances, impact and any repeated behaviour.
Responses may include a request to correct or stop behaviour, removal of
harmful content, a warning, or a temporary or permanent participation
restriction. Serious or repeated harm may justify immediate restrictions.

Maintainers should explain decisions when doing so would not expose private
information or increase harm. Requests to reconsider a decision can use the
same private channel. An uninvolved maintainer should review a concern where
one is available; this small project cannot promise an independent appeal
panel. Platform-level abuse can also be reported through the hosting
platform's own reporting tools.
